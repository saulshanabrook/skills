## Writing guidance

| Source and locator | What the source says | How this skill uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Michael Ernst, *How to write a technical paper or a research paper*, revised July 1, 2024: [Which details to include](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html#details), [Make the organization and results clear](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html#organization) | Explicit advice: give readers the main point early at paper, section, and paragraph level. Emphasize outcomes and significance; implementation effort and research chronology do not determine space. | In empirical results, lead with a supported finding, then explain its evidence. A missing result requires a draft marker, not a fabricated punchline. That handling of pending work is our adaptation. |
| Simon Peyton Jones, *How to Write a Great Research Paper*, [pp.21–24](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf#page=21), [pp.36–39](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf#page=36) | Explicit advice: make contributions specific enough to assess, connect introductory claims to evidence, and convey the intuition through examples before general technical detail. Choose a direct explanation rather than recounting discovery. | Use the claim/evidence map and preserve useful concrete examples. The deck supports the argument behind the structure; its page budgets and forceful placement preferences are not mandatory templates. |
| Jennifer Widom, *Tips for Writing Technical Papers*: [The Body](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/widom/paper-writing.html#body), [Performance Experiments](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/widom/paper-writing.html#experiments), [Appendices](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/widom/paper-writing.html#appendices) | Explicit advice: describe results coherently and explain content top down. Experiments may establish usability, relative performance, sensitivity, or scalability. Material needed to understand the contribution belongs in the main paper. | Give each metric and experiment an argumentative job. Move secondary protocol detail without hiding a comparison condition needed to interpret the result. We do not adopt a fixed introduction paragraph count or restrict every appendix to one content type. |
| Stefanie Tellex, *Writing a Technical Paper*, October 24, 2019: [Technical Approach and Results](https://h2r.cs.brown.edu/writing-a-technical-paper/#:~:text=Technical%20Approach) | Explicit advice: motivate technical material through a hierarchy of problems and solutions. Results should deliver the introduction's promises, use understandable metrics, and keep an experiment's findings near its description. Tellex suggests aim/subhypothesis openers. | Explain why a mechanism is needed before expanding it. Keep local evidence and interpretation together. Her suggested opener differs from Ernst's finding-led emphasis; here we prefer the supported finding in empirical results, while retaining orientation when helpful. |

These guides are advice from individual authors, not a consensus specification. For example, Peyton Jones strongly favors later related work, while Widom gives conditional placement advice and Tellex favors earlier placement but permits later placement. The related-work specialist handles that decision in detail. Do not turn vivid prescriptions about sentence counts, page budgets, passive voice, or citation packages into global rules.

### Guidance addressed to PL audiences

| Source and inspected passage | Explicit advice | Adaptation and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Derek Dreyer, *How to Write Papers So People Can Read Them*, PLMW@POPL 2020: [pp.45,49,56–63,70–73](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~dreyer/talks/talk-plmw20popl.pdf#page=45) | Organize information from familiar context toward new material, keep paragraphs coherent, and present progressively deeper layers. His introduction model connects context, gap, and innovation; a key-ideas section supplies intuition before full technical development. He warns that an unexplained concrete opening can assume too much context. | Make a paper skimmable at several depths while retaining the formal argument. An example needs enough context to be intelligible; it is not automatically the best first sentence. Do not adopt the deck's page budgets or a compulsory standalone overview section. |
| Johnson, Cook, Wirfs-Brock, Booch, Gabriel, and Beck, *How to Get a Paper Accepted at OOPSLA*, OOPSLA'93 panel: [Cook: Theoretical Papers; Gabriel: Programming Language Papers; Beck](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~wcook/papers/HowToGetaPaperAcceptedToOOPSLA/HowToGetAPaperAcceptedToOOPSLA.htm) | The panel distinguishes kinds of contributions and their audiences. Cook asks what a theory lets readers understand or do; Gabriel asks for both implementable detail and an accessible idea; Beck treats a paper as an argument supporting a focused claim. | Match explanation and evidence to the contribution rather than forcing every paper into one evaluation template. This is historical advice from 1993, not current venue policy. Its acceptance statistics, category judgments, and fixed sentence/section prescriptions are not standing requirements. |

## Appendices and evaluation substantiation

Inspected November 13, 2026. Recheck the live venue instructions for the submission's year and stage; the venue example below is not a permanent PL rule.

| Source and locator | Source status and guidance | Adaptation and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [PLDI 2027 CFP](https://pldi27.sigplan.org/track/pldi-2027-papers), “Formatting Requirements” and “Supplementary Material, Mechanized Proofs, and AI Use” | Venue policy: appendices are supplementary and excluded from the text-page limit; reviewers may consult supplements but need not. Proof scripts supporting a main mechanized contribution accompany the submission. | Make the main argument interpretable without requiring supplementary reading. Preserve required proof delivery separately from editorial decisions about explanatory appendix length. This CFP sets no appendix-length target. |
| [SIGPLAN Empirical Evaluation Guidelines](https://www.sigplan.org/Resources/EmpiricalEvaluation/), checklist introduction and FAQ on limitations, benchmark choice, and artifact evaluation | Committee guidance: use evidence appropriate to the claim, expose material limitations, and justify benchmark selection. The checklist supports expert judgment; its examples are not universally applicable. Artifact evaluation checks the paper's claims and evidence against the artifact. | Preserve interpretation-critical experimental conditions while contracting methods. Moving commands and raw logs into the artifact does not replace a comprehensible protocol in the paper. These are methodological recommendations, not a mandatory section template. |
| [Widom, “Appendices”](https://cs.stanford.edu/people/widom/paper-writing.html#appendices) | Individual writing advice: keep the contribution understandable in the main paper and offer detailed substantiation for selective reading. Widom specifically recommends proofs and algorithms. | Apply the reader-access principle; do not adopt the proofs-and-algorithms-only restriction for venues that also permit experimental supplements. Neither the source nor this adaptation supplies a deletion quota. |

## Selected observed paper patterns

These are observations of the cited versions, not writing rules their authors state.

The [full bounded survey](pl-paper-survey.md) covers all sixteen papers in the related-work skill's example corpus, with newly inspected passages beyond related work, versions, locators, and limits. Ruler below is an additional example. Use a relevant record to choose an original passage; the tables do not replace reading that passage when making a new substantive claim.

| Paper and inspected passage | Observed pattern | Reusable adaptation and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nandi et al., *Rewrite Rule Inference Using Equality Saturation* (Ruler), OOPSLA 2021: [§4, pp.13–15](https://ztatlock.net/pubs/2021-oopsla-ruler/2021-oopsla-ruler.pdf#page=13), [§5, pp.15–17](https://ztatlock.net/pubs/2021-oopsla-ruler/2021-oopsla-ruler.pdf#page=15) | The evaluation distinguishes cost, compactness, and derivability. Table 1's caption states a finding. The application study compares absent, existing, synthesized, and combined rules while measuring output quality as well as cost. | Pair semantic capability with resource measures and connect an application to its user's objective. Choose controls that support the causal claim. Do not import the paper's aggregation or workload exclusions, or infer why reviewers liked it. |
| Zhang et al., *Better Together: Unifying Datalog and Equality Saturation* (egglog), PLDI 2023: [§5.3, pp.14–15](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3591239#page=14), [§6.2, pp.18–19](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3591239#page=18) | The microbenchmark explains restrictions needed for comparable scheduling before interpreting time and graph growth. The Herbie study ties interacting analyses to sound rewriting, then reports application accuracy and runtime, including differing outcomes across cases. | Keep a compact fairness condition near its comparison. Explain composed features through a concrete application result and measure the user's outcome. Removing irrelevant caveats does not justify hiding mixed results or deleting a material workload restriction. |
| Willsey et al., *egg: Fast and Extensible Equality Saturation*, arXiv:2004.03082v3, November 7, 2020: [§6 opening and §6.1, pp.20–22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03082v3#page=20) | The case-study opening states the benefits achieved by existing projects. Herbie's application problem and bottleneck lead to the achieved improvement before the implementation subsection. Its results relate simplifier cost to downstream usefulness. | Lead an application narrative with the benefit and problem it solves, then show how the mechanism supports it. Distinguish a component's timing from an end-to-end outcome instead of allowing a large local speedup to imply the same whole-program gain. |
