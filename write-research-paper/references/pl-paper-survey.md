# A bounded survey of PL/CS paper presentation

This covers the same sixteen-paper example corpus as the related-work skill, reading beyond its related-work passages. All sixteen originals were available and selected passages were inspected in November 2026. These are observations and possible adaptations, not advice asserted by the papers, an exhaustive review, or a representative survey of PL norms. No entry implies that every paragraph of a listed section was read. Abstract/introduction openings, named overview passages, and specific evaluation/theorem passages supplied the evidence below.

The versions and public links make the notes usable when this skill is installed alone. Physical PDF pages are used unless the covered-copy offset is explicitly stated. Licensed bundled originals and optional reading copies remain in the sibling skill's [source/rights index](../../write-pl-related-work/references/sources.md#paper-examples); do not duplicate or relicense that library. The index's additional sources and omitted sections are not implicitly part of this reading pass. Ruler is an additional example in [writing-sources.md](writing-sources.md#selected-observed-paper-patterns).

## Choose a pattern for the reader's need

| Reader's need | Relevant examples below |
| --- | --- |
| See a key idea before the full formal construction | eggcc, Aeneas, Declarative Datalog Debugging, RustBelt |
| Understand a new guarantee or why an existing one is insufficient | Provenance Traces, HoTTSQL, RustBelt |
| Assess adoption in existing programs | egg, egglog, ProvSQL |
| Distinguish output quality, semantic capability, and resource cost | Small Proofs, eggcc, Dis/Equality Graphs, Zhao et al. |
| Assess a controlled implementation comparison or timing boundary | Relational E-matching, Lineage Capture Trade-offs, Parallel and Customizable Equality Saturation |
| Interpret prototype evidence at its actual scope | Database Queries that Explain their Work, Aeneas |

## E-graphs, matching, extraction, and proofs

| Source/version and selected passages inspected | Observed presentation | Adaptation and contrasting limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **egg: Fast and Extensible Equality Saturation**, arXiv:2004.03082v3, November 7, 2020. [Introduction opening, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03082v3#page=1); [§6 opening and §6.1, pp.20–22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.03082v3#page=20). | The introduction establishes the reused data structure and application workflow. The case studies state benefits to existing projects before implementation detail; the Herbie result connects the simplifier bottleneck to practical improvement. | Present what an application gained, then the mechanism. Keep a component speedup distinct from whole-application benefit. This organization does not make implementation detail dispensable. |
| **Better Together: Unifying Datalog and Equality Saturation**, PLDI 2023, PACMPL 7, Article 125. [Introduction opening, p.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3591239#page=1); [§5.3, pp.14–15](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3591239#page=14); [§6.2, pp.18–19](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3591239#page=18). | The opening explains the common structure of two traditions. A controlled microbenchmark states schedule-related restrictions; the application study explains interacting analyses through sound rewrites and reports accuracy as well as runtime. | Make the synthesis and user benefit legible, then preserve conditions needed for a valid comparison. A successful combined application does not establish necessity of every component. |
| **Efficient Extraction for Effectful E-graphs**, publication-identifying 2026 copy, PACMPL 10, OOPSLA2, Article 398. [Introduction, pp.1–2](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3839530#page=1); [§3/§3.1 opening, p.5](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3839530#page=5); [§8 opening, p.19](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3839530#page=19). | The introduction identifies a tension between congruence and effect order. The overview illustrates it with a concrete program. Evaluation separates extractor performance, extracted-program quality, and domain-specific usefulness, then states headline answers before setup. | Let the technical obstacle explain the design. Separate tool speed from output quality and application capability; an efficient optimizer can still produce poor programs. |
| **Relational E-matching**, POPL 2022, PACMPL 6, Article 35, copy retaining its corrigendum annotation. [Introduction opening, p.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3498696#page=1); [§5/§5.1 opening and Table 1, pp.16–17](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3498696#page=16). | The introduction motivates matching through its role in applications. The evaluation names the compared implementations, explains workload construction, and distinguishes results including and excluding index construction. | Name the measured operation and cost boundary before interpreting a ratio. This paper's many aggregate statistics are examples, not a reason to add aggregates to a per-workload figure. |
| **Small Proofs from Congruence Closure**, arXiv:2209.03398v1, 2022. [Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.03398v1#page=1); [Figure 8/Table I, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.03398v1#page=7); [Table II, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.03398v1#page=8). | The motivating problem links optimized reasoning to certificates. Comparisons put proof size alongside algorithm cost, and a downstream table distinguishes proof-generation time from total runtime and excluded verification. | Explain both certificate usefulness and its resource cost. Size reduction, generation overhead, and replay savings are different outcomes; retain the relevant boundary rather than replacing all of them with one overhead number. |
| **Dis/Equality Graphs**, POPL 2025, PACMPL 9, Article 77. [Introduction, pp.1–2](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3704913#page=1); [§5 opening, p.15](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3704913#page=15); [§5.1, p.16](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3704913#page=16). | The introduction explains where disequalities arise in real reasoning workflows. Evaluation separates application studies and parameter analysis. The theorem-prover study explains cumulative curves in terms of completed tasks and required resources. | Tie an extension to the task it enables and interpret a plot in those terms. Choose the metric from the question; a cumulative plot is not automatically suitable for a comparison requiring individual workload identity. |
| **Parallel and Customizable Equality Saturation**, CC 2026, 12-page conference copy. [Introduction opening, p.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3771775.3786266#page=1); [§3 opening, p.4](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3771775.3786266#page=4); [§6.1/§7 openings and Table 1, p.7](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3771775.3786266#page=7). | A short overview maps the implementation's ideas to earlier limitations. Four case studies serve different aspects of the design. The memory table explicitly labels Java heap size and another system's resident memory. | Map mechanisms to the problems and case studies that assess them. Label resource definitions and worker settings precisely; different memory measures are not interchangeable merely because both use megabytes. |

## Semantics, verification, and translation

| Source/version and selected passages inspected | Observed presentation | Adaptation and contrasting limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RustBelt: Securing the Foundations of the Rust Programming Language**, 2018 author-hosted publication copy, 34 pages. [§1.2 opening, p.3](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~dreyer/papers/rustbelt/paper.pdf#page=3); [§7 opening, p.28](https://people.mpi-sws.org/~dreyer/papers/rustbelt/paper.pdf#page=28). | The introduction explains the semantic approach and the modeled language. The soundness section interprets judgments, connecting formal contexts and tokens to their proof roles before proceeding. | State what the formal result covers and explain the work done by the notation. A semantics paper can substantiate its central claim by a theorem; it need not imitate a runtime evaluation. |
| **Aeneas: Rust Verification by Functional Translation**, arXiv:2206.07185v2, September 28, 2022, 43 pages. [§2 opening and mutable-borrow translation, pp.4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07185v2#page=4); [§6 hash-table case, p.33](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07185v2#page=33). | Before the formalism, an ordinary Rust function illustrates the functional translation, with its syntax and terminology explained. The case study names a concrete map-behavior property and decomposes its proof into operations and invariants. | Show source behavior and the translated reasoning task before general machinery. Name the property verified; a successful proof example does not by itself establish universal translation correctness. |
| **HoTTSQL: Proving Query Rewrites with Univalent SQL Semantics**, arXiv:1607.04822v2, August 5, 2016. [Introduction opening, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.04822v2#page=1); [§5/§5.1 transition, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.04822v2#page=8). | The introduction connects semantics to query-optimizer correctness. The implementation/evidence passage separates specification, library, and rewrite-proof code; it groups proved rules into categories and uses proof size to describe effort. | Relate formal machinery to the practical correctness question, and choose a size metric with an explicit job. Proof LOC describes one artifact/effort dimension, not runtime cost or a universal usability result. |
| **Provenance Traces — Extended Report**, arXiv:0812.0564v1, December 2, 2008; supplied rendering also prints 2018/10/22. [Abstract opening, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.0564v1#page=1); [fidelity discussion and example, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.0564v1#page=13). | The abstract motivates a semantic account of explanations. A concrete conditional expression then shows why merely recording a consistent result is insufficient to explain execution, motivating the stronger property. | Use a small counterexample to explain why a definition or guarantee is needed. Retain the distinction that carries the argument; not every qualification is dispensable hedging. |

## Debugging, provenance, and application evidence

| Source/version and selected passages inspected | Observed presentation | Adaptation and contrasting limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Declarative Datalog Debugging for Mere Mortals**, 2012, 12-page author PDF. [§1 opening and running example, pp.1–2](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~ludaesch/pubs/DeclarativeDebugging.pdf#page=1); [§4 opening, p.9](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~ludaesch/pubs/DeclarativeDebugging.pdf#page=9). | A small transitive-closure program makes the debugging problem concrete. Its input, output, derivation graph, and highlighted goal are explained together before the provenance rewriting is generalized. The implementation discussion returns to the method's declarative contract. | Keep an example's semantic role beside its code and diagram. Visual highlighting should identify the goal or transformation, not merely decorate syntax. One sufficient example can be better than an inventory of features. |
| **Debugging Large-scale Datalog: A Scalable Provenance Evaluation Strategy**, TOPLAS 42(2), Article 7, April 2020. [Introduction opening, pp.1–2](https://souffle-lang.github.io/pdf/toplas20.pdf#page=1); [§5 opening, p.25](https://souffle-lang.github.io/pdf/toplas20.pdf#page=25). | The introduction connects declarative semantics to an actual debugging difficulty. The experiment section identifies three distinct claims: evaluation overhead, interactive exploration scalability, and the size of proof trees motivating that interaction. | Make each empirical claim assessable with an appropriate observation. A resource result can motivate an interface or capability rather than being an isolated benchmark statistic; not every claim needs its own subsection. |
| **Database Queries that Explain their Work**, arXiv:1408.1675v3, August 12, 2014; inspected Edinburgh covered copy. Abstract opening on covered p.2 ([official p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1675v3#page=1)); §7 passage on covered p.12 ([official p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.1675v3#page=11)). | The abstract connects explanation and reproducibility to semantic properties. The implementation contrasts eager/lazy tracing and explicitly explains what the simplified workflow measurement does and does not model. | Link implementation choices to the property and cost they affect. Keep a workload-model boundary when it changes interpretation; do not promote a prototype experiment into established production performance. |
| **Lineage Capture Trade-offs: A Case Study in DuckDB**, ProvenanceWeek 2025, five pages. [Introduction opening, p.1](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3736229.3736265#page=1); [§3/§3.1 opening, p.3](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3736229.3736265#page=3). | The comparison is motivated by prior approaches having been evaluated in different systems. A common engine supports the comparison; operator microbenchmarks isolate mechanisms behind full-query overhead. | Explain what the experimental control makes comparable, then use focused measurements to interpret the headline result. An explanatory microbenchmark supplements rather than substitutes for the application measurement. |
| **ProvSQL: A General System for Keeping Track of the Provenance and Probability of Data**, arXiv:2504.12058v3, December 17, 2025, journal reference ICDE 2026. [Abstract opening, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.12058v3#page=1); [benchmark rationale, pp.7–8 and Tables II–III, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.12058v3#page=7). | The abstract ties supported query/data models to system design and performance. The benchmark table records language-feature coverage; another distinguishes successful execution, unsupported queries, timeouts, and memory failures across systems. | Put capability/coverage evidence beside cost, with provenance for the inputs and applicable comparisons. Do not let a success-only timing subset imply support for the full inventory. |

## Limits of the survey

The selected passages support several viable presentations: a result-led empirical section, an example-led technical overview, a context-led introduction, and a theorem-led formal argument. They do not establish mandatory section counts, a universal chart type, a fixed amount of mathematics, or a single acceptable prose style. Formal proofs, successful application checks, benchmark counts, and runtime measurements remain different kinds of evidence. Reopen the relevant original and inspect the changed claim's context before using these notes for a new factual comparison.
