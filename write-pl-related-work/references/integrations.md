# Optional research integrations and focused subtasks

Use integrations to close an identified evidence gap, not to satisfy a ritual. Existing bibliography entries, supplied PDFs, and primary public sources may be sufficient. Discover the tools and installed skills actually available; do not invent capabilities or assume a library/account is connected.

## Paperpile

When available and useful, load the applicable installed Paperpile skill for the bounded research subtask:

- **deep-library-search:** before searching the user's Paperpile library, follow its instructions for finding relevant saved sources.
- **find-papers:** discovery, exact papers, or citation-chain questions.
- **auto-highlight-papers:** text/PDF access and close reading when supported by the integration. Permission to read does not imply permission to highlight, save, tag, or annotate.
- **narrative-paper-survey:** a requested longer research synthesis. Its research deliverable conventions do not dictate the manuscript's paragraph structure or LaTeX format.

Use only the relevant subskill, not all of them by default. Follow its actual documentation and required research-output format. Keep those research notes separate from the user's requested manuscript artifact. When access is unavailable, explain that once and continue with a read-only primary-source fallback where possible; do not repeat unauthorized retries.

## Litmaps

Read the actual available tool documentation; do not refer to an invented Litmaps skill. Start from exact, disambiguated seed papers. When expansion is useful, use citation-connected search with multiple relevant seeds, inspect selected original sources, and do another bounded round only if the evidence changes the selection or leaves a material gap.

Deduplicate duplicate records of the same work and version using DOI and version metadata. Retain distinct versions when their claims, pagination, or publication status differ, and identify which version supports the comparison. A citation-connected rank measures a retrieval relationship, not historical priority, causal lineage, or substantive similarity. A map helps find sources; their full text supports the claims.

Show selected results as a Litmap whenever the actual tool's output contract requires it, even if the user requested source discovery rather than a saved map. Use unsaved display when persistence is not authorized. Save, add, or annotate only within the user's authorization. For authorized writes, reuse an existing map/account when its identity is clear; clarify the missing write target when necessary. A read-only source search does not authorize saving a map or modifying the library.

## Focused delegation

Delegate independent evidence questions when useful. No fixed agent count, model, or mandatory review stage is needed. A useful brief names:

- the manuscript claim or design decision to clarify;
- the owned source family or exact sources, avoiding duplicate scans;
- the relevant comparison axis and permitted side effects;
- a stop condition proportional to the gap.

Ask for a compact packet: source/version and verified metadata, actual mechanism, relationship to the manuscript, exact section/page or other evidence pointer, caveat, and the strongest supported claim. Separate an author's explicit comparison from the researcher's interpretation. The coordinating writer synthesizes the packets and resolves conflicting readings against sources; it should not concatenate paper summaries.

Example brief:

> Read these two typed-compiler papers to establish whether they support staged lowering through distinct intermediate languages. Return each stage's role, the exact preservation result, whether it is mechanized, and the limit of the analogy. Read-only; stop once those claims are supported. Do not expand into a general verified-compilers survey.

Independent source or draft review can help when a material uncertainty remains. Review the actual current wording, and avoid repeated critique of an obsolete draft. Do not contact authors, post reviews, or publish manuscript/service changes merely because a writing guide recommends expert feedback.
