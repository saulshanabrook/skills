# Simon Peyton Jones — research-paper slides

**Source/version:** [How to Write a Great Research Paper](https://simon.peytonjones.org/great-research-paper/#introduction); current linked [52-page PDF](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf), checked November 10, 2026. The URL's upload year is not asserted as the talk's date; PDF metadata mislabels the deck.

**Read:** physical [pp.27–30](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf#page=27) (placement), [pp.31–32](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf#page=31) (credit), and [pp.41–44](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/uploads/prod/2016/07/How-to-write-a-great-research-paper.pdf#page=41) (feedback).

**Explicit advice:** Explain the idea before compressed technical comparisons; credit predecessors generously and acknowledge weaknesses. Ask readers to check understanding and fairness; repair the paper after criticism.

**Adaptation/limits:** A foundation is a useful relationship, not merely a competitor to discredit. Later placement is the deck's preference, not a universal rule supported by all four guides. Seeking feedback does not authorize outreach.
