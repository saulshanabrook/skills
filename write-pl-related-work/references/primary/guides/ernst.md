# Michael Ernst — writing advice

**Source/version:** [How to write a technical paper or a research paper](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html), April 2005; page revised July 1, 2024. Checked November 10, 2026.

**Read:** [comparison](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html#related-work), [reader goal](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html#message), [organization](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html#organization), and [feedback](https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~mernst/advice/write-technical-paper.html#feedback).

**Explicit advice:** Explain contributions, relationships among predecessors, and differences from the present work. A collection of abstract summaries is insufficient. Orient readers before detail. Ernst generally prefers late related work, while acknowledging alternative orders. Use misunderstandings to improve the paper; ordinarily apply one reader's corrections before requesting another review.

**Adaptation/limits:** Organize final prose around the relationships that clarify the manuscript. This does not prohibit paper-by-paper research notes, close-predecessor paragraphs, or early positioning. The guide supports revising explanations after feedback; persistent skill updates and their authorization are separate workflow decisions.
