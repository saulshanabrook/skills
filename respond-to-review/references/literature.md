# Research behind this skill

This skill combines established ideas; it is not a separately validated communication protocol. The sources below support particular parts. Their translation into an agent workflow is our design synthesis.

## Dialogue and design

**Clark & Brennan (1991), Grounding in Communication.** Communication involves establishing evidence of understanding adequate for the current purpose. Their principle of least collaborative effort concerns the participants' combined work. This motivates a targeted reply that supplies the missing distinction without making the reviewer reconstruct the argument. It does not require exhaustive agreement or explicit confirmation after every message. [Author-hosted chapter](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan%2C%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf).

**Eyal, Steffel & Epley (2018), Perspective Mistaking.** Across 25 experiments, imagining another's perspective did not consistently improve interpersonal accuracy; their final experiment found improvement from obtaining perspective through conversation. Treat inferred assumptions as hypotheses and seek information when the distinction matters. These experiments concern interpersonal predictions, not code review; they do not show that every clarification question is useful or that people can fully report their assumptions. [Author-university publication and abstract](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/perspective-mistaking-accurately-understanding-the-mind-of-anothe-2/), DOI: 10.1037/pspa0000115.

**Schön (1992), Designing as Reflective Conversation with the Materials of a Design Situation.** The same artifact can be interpreted through different selected features, descriptions, rules, and criteria. Investigating consequences can change a designer's framing and intentions. This motivates trying the reviewer's alternative and revising our own model, rather than merely translating a fixed design into clearer language. The paper uses qualitative design examples; it does not establish a universal procedure. [Publisher](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01580516), [university-hosted full text](https://creativetech.mat.ucsb.edu/readings/Schon1992_Article_DesigningAsReflectiveConversat.pdf).

**Schegloff (1992), Repair After Next Turn.** Responses display how preceding conduct was understood, allowing participants to recognize and repair misunderstandings. A follow-up review comment is therefore useful evidence about how our previous response landed. This is a transfer from ordinary conversation, not a claim that its exact timing rules hold in asynchronous PR threads. [Publisher abstract and metadata](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/229903).

## Code review

**Bacchelli & Bird (2013), Expectations, Outcomes, and Challenges of Modern Code Review.** This Microsoft field study identifies code/change understanding as a central review challenge, with outcomes including knowledge transfer and alternative solutions. It supports taking questions about rationale seriously, rather than measuring review solely by defect fixes. It does not test this skill's response method. [Official publication](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/expectations-outcomes-and-challenges-of-modern-code-review/).

**Sadowski et al. (2018), Modern Code Review: A Case Study at Google.** Interview findings describe breakdowns involving expectations about design-review scope and missing change context, alongside survey and large-scale log analysis. This motivates checking whether participants are evaluating the same decision and scope. The qualitative reports are not causal estimates derived from every review in the log dataset. [Official publication](https://research.google/pubs/modern-code-review-a-case-study-at-google/), [paper](https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/4476.pdf).

**Google Engineering Practices, How to handle reviewer comments.** This practitioner guidance recommends understanding the request, collaborative discussion of alternatives and tradeoffs, and clarifying code or durable code comments when confusion would recur. It is especially useful for distinguishing disagreement about consequences from disagreement about how to weight them. It is guidance, not an experimental result. [Official guide](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/developer/handling-comments.html). The [review standard](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/standard.html) additionally permits multiple valid designs and favors technical facts over personal preference.

## Questions that develop understanding

**Argyris, Putnam & McLain Smith (1985), Action Science.** Collaborative inquiry can expose tacit knowledge, generate alternatives, and reconsider ends as well as means. Make your own position and observable basis inspectable, then invite challenge; do not conceal an argument inside leading questions. This supports dialogue that can change either participant's framing. The work is an organizational inquiry framework, not PR-specific experimental evidence. [Book and author-hosted chapters](https://actiondesign.com/resources/readings/action-science), especially [chapter 8](https://actiondesign.com/assets/pdf/AScha8.pdf), pp. 236–238, 245–246, 251–261. [Chapter 3](https://actiondesign.com/assets/pdf/AScha3.pdf), pp. 82–83, cautions that stated accounts need not reveal tacit patterns; [chapter 10](https://actiondesign.com/assets/pdf/AScha10.pdf), pp. 361–362, shows why appending an agreement question to an unexplained opinion is insufficient inquiry.

**Padesky (1993), Socratic Questioning: Changing Minds or Guiding Discovery?** This invited keynote describes inquiry through questioning, listening, summarizing, and synthesis, with movement between concrete examples and broader implications. Answers can redirect the questioner; questions should help discovery, not guide someone to a predetermined conclusion. We borrow that reciprocal openness and use of examples, not a therapist–patient stance or an inflexible sequence. This is clinical practitioner guidance, not evidence that this review method has been experimentally validated. [Author-hosted keynote](https://padesky.com/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/socquest.pdf), especially pp. 3–6.

**Eris (2003), Asking Generative Design Questions: A Fundamental Cognitive Mechanism in Design Thinking.** The paper distinguishes questions seeking existing answers from design questions that generate possibilities, scenarios, or alternative methods. The latter can help develop requirements and ideas neither person already has. We use this distinction when choosing whether to clarify a premise or jointly explore a possibility. The taxonomy draws on design observations; its proposed links to design performance remained hypotheses, not proof that particular questions improve PR outcomes. [Official conference paper](https://www.designsociety.org/download-publication/24150/asking_generative_design_questions_a_fundamental_cognitive_mechanism_in_design_thinking), sections 3–5.

**Kensing & Munk-Madsen (1992), Participatory Design: Structure in the Toolbox.** This participatory-design paper distinguishes knowledge about present work, technological options, and a proposed system, including concrete and abstract forms. It informs the choice of shared examples, prototypes, or conceptual discussion according to what participants need to learn. It is background for selecting inquiry methods, not a mandatory taxonomy for every reply. This is the PDC 1992 paper, not the related 1993 CACM article. [Conference paper](https://ojs.ruc.dk/index.php/pdc/article/view/77/69).

Google's reciprocal [Handling pushback](https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/pushback.html) guidance also asks reviewers to consider whether an author may be right. We use it as practitioner support for inquiry in both directions, rather than treating the author as the only person who might revise a view.

## Choosing the text

**Diátaxis, Daniele Procida.** Its explanation guidance emphasizes bounded why-questions, context, alternatives, and examples. Its compass distinguishes understanding, precise information, practical accomplishment, and learning through action. Use these distinctions to choose what the reviewer needs; a short example in a reply is usually explanation rather than a tutorial. This skill applies the framework at the scale of a reply and its supporting artifacts, rather than treating every conversation as a four-part documentation project. [Explanation](https://diataxis.fr/explanation/), [compass](https://diataxis.fr/compass/).

## Our synthesis

The practical loop is: identify a possible difference in assumptions or goals; choose a useful question, explanation, or experiment; let what emerges revise the interpretation or design; then choose the next turn. Questions can develop goals and possibilities rather than merely retrieve known information. The next reply should contain only what advances the current conversation; an explanation is not required at every turn.

An experiment can distinguish behavior predictions. It cannot decide which stakeholder goal should dominate. Likewise, an inferred mental model is not a measured fact, and sending a good explanation is not proof that understanding or agreement has been achieved. A response can legitimately end with a bounded disagreement or one discriminating question.

Sources checked October 31, 2026. Routine skill use does not require reopening these sources.
